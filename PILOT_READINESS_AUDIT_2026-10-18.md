# Pilot readiness audit: natural agent-coding study

**Operational release gate for the feasibility phase.** The main-study method is explained in the [A–Z study guide](STUDY_GUIDE.md). The pilot uses its same two-task structure and tests the materials before main recruitment; this audit is not an alternative research protocol.

**18 October 2026 — local working audit. No participant findings.** This checks the current `natural-2026-10-13-r3` workflow and the new `2026-10-18-api-contrast-v1` task packages. The hosted portal has a prior synthetic two-task check; the RQ-linked analytics and this audit are local and have not been deployed. Previously issued ZIPs remain frozen. The initial proposal and controlled-checkpoint files are historical, not instructions for this pilot.

## Decision

**Self-rehearsal can continue. The repository does not yet document enough evidence to clear another real-user pilot on the new task revision.** Clear the four P0 gates below on the exact intended host and portal before inviting a participant. A pilot does *not* have to produce vulnerabilities or statistically significant condition differences; it is meant to discover whether the task and measurements work. Keep any already collected rehearsal or pilot records with their actual package/form versions, rather than retroactively treating them as the new version.

Record each gate result, revision and evidence location in the [pilot release record template](study-system/pilot/NATURAL_PILOT_GATE_RECORD.md); it is blank until the checks are performed.

The research comparison is between **assigned work-structure packages** (participant guidance plus familiar mapped code versus a small unfamiliar interface), with **observed** acceleration/exploration coded separately. The outcome is confidence about final SQL/path feature security, independently assessed final security, reliance and verification. It cannot identify a pure cognitive-state effect, an AI-versus-no-AI effect, or a stable personal trust trait.

## RQ-to-evidence audit

| Question | Current evidence path | What must be refined or checked in the pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RQ1: confidence aligned with final security** | Two 0–100 final-feature probabilities tied to the final hash; independent SQL/path review; participant-paired Brier and secure/vulnerable confidence summaries; separately coded observed mode. | Check that participants understand “meets its security requirement,” especially if a feature is unfinished. The rating includes their own edits, so call it *final-code security confidence*, not a direct agent-trust probability. With only four items/person, report group-level probability accuracy and reliance context, not individual calibration curves. |
| **RQ2: final vulnerabilities** | All assigned features, including not implemented/unassessable; separate functionality/security labels; confirmed agent-origin exposures only where video/transcript/code show them. | Test the reference cases; quantify actual insecure agent exposure. Final vulnerability does not prove agent causation or conscious acceptance. If exposure is rare, the final-security comparison remains answerable but exposure retention may be unestimable. Do not plant a flaw in the natural task merely to fill a denominator. |
| **RQ3: verification and retention** | Resolved human check events with actor, purpose, viewed result, class and timing; agent application, inspection, continuation, repair and final status; `coding_status.csv` distinguishes zero from not coded. | Pilot episode boundaries and a defensible “continued with change” anchor. Direct agent edits have no native accept click. Double-code pilot recordings; keep coder drafts, boundary disagreements and adjudication. A check–outcome association is observational because skill, suspicion and difficulty may affect both. |
| **RQ4: SQL versus path** | Both classes in both tasks, with class-specific security outcomes and counts. | Validate path fixtures, symlink support and SQL data flow in both familiar and interface versions. Report assessable denominators and sparse-event limits. The 2024 MITRE Top 25 ranks CWE-89 #3 and CWE-22 #5, which supports importance, **not** LLM-specific prevalence. |

## Instrument and task checks during the first 4–6 feasibility sessions

1. **Manipulation and difficulty:** Code active intent episodes without using condition, speed, verification or security outcome as labels. Compare observed mode shares and the portal's known-step/option-seeking answers, while reporting unclear/mixed time. Also compare functional completion, active time, perceived difficulty, help, exposure to agent output and the two projects across the four sequences. Barke et al. motivate the known-step versus approach-seeking construct, but do not validate this compound card/interface manipulation.
2. **Questionnaire comprehension and priming:** The natural pre-task form has **nine visible items**, not the eleven in the older controlled guide. The after-task form's explicit known-step and option-seeking questions are shown after Task 1 and may cue Task 2. The first security rating may also heighten later checking. Ask about these effects after both tasks; decide whether to retain or revise the approved wording *between* pilot versions. Do not use self-reported mode as ground truth. See the natural questionnaire map.
3. **Early-career interpretation:** Retain role, paid experience, JavaScript/SQL/file familiarity, security training and agent experience. An insecure final feature can reflect knowledge or difficulty as well as reliance. The current natural pre-task form intentionally hides direct target-attack recognition items to reduce priming, so it cannot objectively separate security skill from trust. Do not claim it does. Consider a non-target baseline skill measure for the main study only after pilot/ethics review; otherwise present the limitation and replay evidence.
4. **Task security salience:** Both task briefs and agent guidance explicitly require SQL input to remain data and file access inside a root. This is a security-salient setting and may suppress insecure output; it is not a measure of wholly unprompted vulnerability awareness. Keep those requirements equal across conditions and check the agent really sees equivalent target criteria.
5. **Interview quality:** Use the new natural replay guide after both tasks. Pick clips by a fixed event rule, including no-check and failed cases. Elicit spontaneous intent and security belief before showing a prior rating or revealing the hidden verdict. Compare hindsight explanations with contemporaneous traces.
6. **Reviewer/coder workload and reliability:** Time one full evidence audit, two independent final-code reviews, two independent behavioural passes and reconciliation. Report pre-resolution agreement and label prevalence. Episode *unitization* must be compared before agreement on labels; an agreement statistic over already reconciled rows is misleading. Freeze the practical double-coding plan before main recruitment.

## What to decide after, not before, the feasibility pilot

| Observation | Defensible response |
| --- | --- |
| Little observed difference between assigned conditions | Revise the work-structure stimulus and pilot the new version, or narrow the claim to assigned package differences. Never relabel assignment as observed mode. |
| Exploration substantially harder or slower | Revise the interface/task and rerun a separate pilot; do not explain a security contrast as mode alone. |
| No confirmed insecure agent exposures | Report that retention among exposed changes is not estimable. Keep final-security and confidence analyses. A guaranteed-exposure experiment would be a separate protocol. |
| Many unimplemented or unassessable final features | Report them explicitly; investigate task cap/comprehension and whether the primary complete-pair estimate is too selective. Predefine a missing-data sensitivity before main recruitment. |
| Ambiguous checks or weak capture | Improve event definitions/capture and repeat the pilot; do not score unread agent tests as human verification. |

## Literature support and original elements

- [Barke et al., *Grounded Copilot*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15000): observed acceleration as knowing a next step and exploration as seeking an approach, including familiar/unfamiliar task components. Our agent-mode conditions and codebook are adaptations.
- [Perry et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.03622): security outcomes and participants' beliefs can diverge under AI assistance. Our exact-final-hash probability instrument and within-person contrast are original.
- [Khalid et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21020): developers' selection, evaluation and final edits of AI code are researchable security processes. Our free agent editing and before/after continuation coding differ from their controlled suggestions.
- [Mozannar et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14306): retrospective programming-activity labels need trace context; accepting a suggestion omits later verification work. Our video/transcript/snapshot triangulation and no-event status are original rules.
- [OWASP Secure Code Review](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Secure_Code_Review_Cheat_Sheet.html): manual data-flow review complements tests and static tools. It does not validate any one scanner as ground truth.
- [Gneiting and Raftery](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437), [Lakens](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267), and [Krippendorff](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.2004.tb00738.x): support proper probability scoring, explicit sample-size justification and careful coding reliability. Thirty completed participants is a resource-bounded target; RQ4 and check associations remain exploratory.

None of these sources validates the complete task, questionnaire, Antigravity observer or causal cognitive-mode claim. The pilot supplies the needed local evidence. Freeze the final RQs, primary paired contrast, exclusions and analysis after the pilot and before main recruitment.
