# Pilot cohort and recruitment draft

Check these criteria against the institution's approved protocol before inviting anyone. The cohort supports claims about **early-career JavaScript-capable developers**, not all professional developers or everyone new to AI.

## Inclusion

- Age and consent capacity as stated in the approved ethics application.
- Final-year CS/IT/software-engineering student **or** junior software developer with at most two years of paid software-development experience.
- Able to read and edit a small JavaScript/Node project; a short self-report or neutral practice confirms this.
- Willing and able to use the designated coding agent and complete both tasks with consented recording.

## Exclusion and measurement

Exclude direct contributors to the study tasks, candidate pool or hidden oracles, and anyone who has seen the answer key. Do not exclude a person solely for prior AI-coding use unless the approved protocol specifies an experience ceiling. Record IDE, agent, JavaScript and security experience in the pre-task form. Analyze or describe these as possible explanations for misses; do not equate a missed vulnerability with overtrust. [Kaur](../../REFERENCES.md) supports transparent security-study sampling and [Danilova](../../REFERENCES.md) supports screening actual programming experience.

## Invitation text for approval

“We invite final-year computing students and junior software developers to a study of coding-agent use in two small JavaScript tasks. The session includes code review, short questionnaires, a recorded coding session and an interview. Estimated duration is approximately two hours [replace after rehearsal]. Agent-generated changes may contain security weaknesses. Participation is voluntary and unrelated to coursework or employment assessment. For full information, compensation and contact details, see the institution-approved participant sheet.”

## Pilot allocation and reporting

Enroll four consented people first. Audit that the four project/condition/order sequences are covered. Candidate-status profiles and checkpoint rotations belong only to the old controlled study; they are not natural-task exposures. Keep rehearsal, pilot and main allocation records separate. Six participants cover the first four plus two more sequences but are not a fully balanced block. Do not choose or swap an assignment after seeing candidate status. Record approached, screened, eligible, consented, started, completed and withdrawn counts, plus reasons for missing task data where allowed by consent. The pilot is for feasibility and wording, not an RQ significance test.
